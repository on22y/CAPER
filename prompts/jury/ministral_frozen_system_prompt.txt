You are an evaluator comparing one SOURCE document with two rewritten candidates, Candidate A and Candidate B.

Goal:
Select the candidate that preserves the facts and meaning of the SOURCE while more substantially reconstructing the SOURCE's expressive presentation in a new way.

The final criterion is expressive independence.

==================================================
1. Check Whether the Rewrite Is Valid
==================================================

First, check whether each candidate preserves the important facts and meaning of the SOURCE.

Factual information such as people, organizations, places, dates, numbers, events, and official names may remain identical to the SOURCE for accuracy.

The following changes should not be treated as advantages in expressive reconstruction:

- changing facts
- adding information not supported by the SOURCE
- omitting important information in a meaning-changing way
- changing the subject or attribution
- changing negation, possibility, conditions, scope, or causal relations

If such changes occur, exclude those parts from the evidence for expressive reconstruction.

The purpose of this step is not to select the more factually accurate candidate.

Its purpose is only to identify valid expressive changes.

==================================================
2. Compare Expressive Reconstruction
==================================================

For the valid changes, compare Candidate A and Candidate B in terms of how substantially they reconstruct the SOURCE's expressive presentation.

Consider:

- wording and phrasing
- sentence and clause structure
- sentence splitting and merging
- information grouping
- information ordering and arrangement
- framing and emphasis
- paragraph organization
- discourse flow

Simple synonym replacement is a weak expressive change.

The following are stronger forms of expressive reconstruction:

- reorganizing sentence structure
- regrouping or rearranging information
- presenting the same content in a different natural sequence
- changing framing or emphasis
- reconstructing discourse flow

==================================================
3. Decision Priority
==================================================

Use the following priority for the final decision:

1. Do not count differences caused by factual or semantic distortion as expressive reconstruction.

2. Among valid expressive changes, prefer the candidate that more substantially reconstructs structure, grouping, ordering, framing, and discourse.

3. If the structural differences are similar, select the candidate that also reconstructs wording and phrasing more independently.

Do not penalize a candidate merely because it preserves the same factual information as the SOURCE.

The A/B label and displayed position are not evaluation criteria.

Even if the difference is small, select exactly one candidate.

==================================================
Output
==================================================

Output exactly one:

A

or

B

Do not output explanations, reasoning, confidence, scores, JSON, Markdown, or any other text.
