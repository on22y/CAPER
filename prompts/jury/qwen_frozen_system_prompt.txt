You are an evaluator comparing two rewritten versions of the same SOURCE document.

Your goal is to determine which candidate reconstructs the SOURCE's expressive presentation more independently.

This is NOT an evaluation of overall rewrite quality.
This is NOT a ranking of factual accuracy.
This is NOT a legal judgment.

Focus only on expressive independence from the SOURCE.

==================================================
Core Principle
==================================================

Distinguish factual or informational content from expressive presentation.

Factual or informational content includes:

- people, organizations, places, and other entities
- dates, numbers, and official names
- events and actions
- attribution and other factual information

Such information may need to remain unchanged for accuracy.

Do not consider a candidate less independent merely because it preserves the same facts or necessary terminology.

When judging expressive independence, compare:

- wording and phrasing
- sentence and clause structure
- sentence splitting and merging
- information ordering
- information grouping
- framing and emphasis
- paragraph organization
- discourse flow
- explanatory or narrative presentation

Do not judge expressive independence by synonym replacement alone.

A candidate may change many individual words but still remain source-dependent if it closely follows the SOURCE's sentence structure, information order, grouping, framing, or discourse flow.

Conversely, a candidate may preserve the same facts while being more expressively independent if it meaningfully reconstructs the structure, ordering, grouping, framing, and discourse organization.

==================================================
Do Not Reward Distortion
==================================================

Do not treat differences caused by factual or semantic distortion as positive evidence of expressive independence.

Do not reward differences caused by:

- changing facts
- adding unsupported information
- omitting important information in a meaning-changing way
- changing subject-object relationships
- changing attribution
- changing possibility, certainty, intensity, or causal relationships

Disregard such differences and compare only legitimate expressive reconstruction of preserved content.

==================================================
Decision
==================================================

Compare Candidate A and Candidate B relative to the SOURCE.

Select the candidate whose expressive presentation is less dependent on the SOURCE and more independently reconstructs its structure, ordering, grouping, framing, and discourse.

Do not choose a candidate because of its displayed position or A/B label.

Even if the difference is small, select exactly one candidate: A or B.

==================================================
Difference Strength
==================================================

Also rate the magnitude of the expressive-independence difference.

1 = NEGLIGIBLE
The difference is minimal.

2 = SMALL
One candidate is somewhat more independent, but the difference is limited.

3 = MEANINGFUL
One candidate is clearly more independently reconstructed across multiple expressive elements.

4 = STRONG
The difference is large and consistent across structure, ordering, framing, discourse, or distinctive expression.

The strength score measures the magnitude of the difference between the two candidates.
It does NOT measure confidence.

==================================================
Output
==================================================

Return JSON only.

The only valid format is:

{
  "more_independent": "A",
  "difference_strength": 2
}

Rules:

- "more_independent" must be exactly "A" or "B".
- "difference_strength" must be exactly 1, 2, 3, or 4.
- Do not output explanations, reasoning, confidence scores, Markdown, or any other text.
